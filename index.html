Action mapping that kills courses early

AI / Performance

Course requests dropped to last resort; doc fixes and product feedback got real owners.

Problem. Course requests arrived already solved. An early AI design skill made it worse by assuming training was always the right solution.

What I built. A triage skill that runs before design work. Goal, then behaviors, then obstacles, then the smallest fix. Seven outcomes: course, live training, doc fix, product feedback, in-product help, extend existing course, or hold.

What didn't work first. The skill checked goals were observable but never checked measurement. Only one audience allowed. No path to "this shouldn't be a course."

Where a human decides. The team confirms the action map together; the skill can send a map back with a stated reason instead of quietly re-asking.

Fixing AI writing at the source

AI / Quality

Eval output went from 15 PASS/FAIL lines to a short problem list.

Problem. Lesson drafts sounded machine-written even though a grader caught them.

What I built. Found the drafting instructions were the problem. Rewrote bans to be absolute, added real before/after voice examples, cut the grader from 15 checks to 4.

What didn't work first. Grading harder with a 15-check checklist did nothing. First test was circular: examples came from the draft being graded.

Where a human decides. Only "Fix" items block publishing. SME-approved text is skipped.

Decision rights in an AI pipeline

Governance

SME opinions flowed as evidence; curriculum calls stayed with Education.

Problem. Meeting notes flattened SME opinions as settled scope.

What I built. A "Who decides what" rule in every design/drafting skill: SME input is evidence or an open question, Education decides what gets made.

Shows. Governance and human-in-the-loop design — the part of AI adoption most teams skip.

Running 15 AI skills like a product

Systems

Teammates always on current versions without touching files; one teammate contributed two skills.

Problem. 15 skills, shared references that broke when zipped, silent backup failures, same course data copied into three places.

What I built. Shared references with a sync script, one-command publishing with version stamps, auto-commit on every edit (522 commits in 12 weeks), field-ownership model, automated deck checks.

Shows. I can run an AI system after launch: versioning, retirement, rollback.

Onboarding: evidence over conclusions

Analytics

Correct null result: partner-led ~ company-led (LMS-active 58.3% vs 61.0%, certified 10.7% vs 14.3%).

Problem. Leadership wanted to know whether partner-led onboarding produced more LMS activity and certification than company-led.

What I built. Analysis across 180 companies, rebuilt twice. First version joined by company name (missed 9 companies). Fixed with ID-based join and timing-controlled cohort.

Result. Corrected a conclusion that would have steered program investment.
